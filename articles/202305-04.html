Blurring the Line between Reality and Virtuality: Innovative Geolocation Gaming Ideas

Unlocking Your Adventure with Geolocation-Based Games

Ossian Lin Published on May 2023

Geolocation-based video games, also known as location-based games, have a relatively short but fascinating history. Back to 2013, Ingress was released by Niantic. It’s the first geolocation-based video games and the game was a precursor to Niantic’s later success with Pokémon Go. In fact, the prototype of Geolocation-based games was developed by a group of students at New York University in 2004, which calls Pac-Manhattan.

Even after 19 years, there are still many similar games being released each year. Capcom and Niantic have recently announced the release of a new augmented reality game based on the Monster Hunter franchise. While the continued release of similar games suggests that there may be potential for innovation and growth in this space, it is also possible that it is simply a result of hype and that true innovation has yet to occur.

Forging a New Path: The Legacy of Original Game Design

When it comes to geolocation-based games, many people think of Ingress. However, the first prototype of this type of game was actually Pac-Manhattan, which was created by students at New York University’s Interactive Telecommunications Program. The game was played on the streets of Manhattan, with real buildings and landmarks serving as the walls of the maze. Players communicated with each other using cell phones, and the game was coordinated by a team of volunteers who tracked the players’ movements and controlled the behavior of the ghosts.

Ingress offers a more advanced and polished gameplay experience compared to Pac-Manhattan. Players use their smartphone and GPS to interact with virtual objects that appear in real-world locations around them. Ingress offers a user-friendly and streamlined gameplay experience that is easy to pick up and understand.

The success of Ingress played a significant role in inspiring the development of Pokémon Go, which was released in 2016 by Niantic. By introducing augmented reality technology to allow players to catch virtual Pokemon overlaid on the real world using their smartphones, Pokémon Go redefined the geolocation-based gaming experience. While Ingress focused on a science-fiction storyline, Pokémon Go tapped into the hugely popular Pokemon franchise and used its familiar characters and gameplay mechanics to attract a wider audience.

Pokémon Go quickly became a massive hit, attracting millions of players around the world and generating billions of dollars in revenue. The game’s success helped to bring geolocation-based games and AR technology into the mainstream, and it inspired the development of other similar games like Harry Potter: Wizards Unite, Minecraft Earth and Pikmin Bloom.

Collaborating with well-known brands or franchises can certainly help to increase the success. Popular brands can help to attract a wider audience and create a more engaging and immersive experience for players. However, it is not a guaranteed success for everyone. In 2021, several games with popular brands such as Harry Potter: Wizards Unite, Minecraft Earth, and The Witcher: Monster Slayer were shut down, due in part to the impact of COVID-19 on people’s ability to go outside. Despite the potential benefits, there are inherent challenges associated with developing these types of games.

Navigating the Fun: Enhancing the Gaming Experience

The success of Pokémon Go paved the way for many geolocation-based games that share a similar gameplay mechanic, allowing players to explore the real world and interact with virtual objects using their mobile devices. Augmented reality has the potential to greatly improve the experience by adding virtual objects and elements to the real world, creating a more immersive and interactive experience for players. This can provide a more accurate and realistic representation of the game world, making the game feel more like an extension of the real world.

While many geolocation-based games share similar gameplay mechanics, it’s important for developers to continue innovating and improving to keep players engaged and avoid monotony. One of the way to achieve this is by incorporating different gameplay elements, such as First Person Shooter mechanics, which can enhance the player experience and keep them engaged.

In the first Watch Dogs game, Cash Run and NVZN were mini-games that seamlessly blended the virtual and real world(still in game). Cash Run challenges players to collect coins while navigating obstacles in the real world, while NVZN requires players to defend against alien attacks overlaid onto their surroundings. These innovative uses of AR technology and engaging gameplay mechanics demonstrate how combining virtual and real-world elements can enhance the overall gaming experience.

Level Up Your Gaming: Innovations in Next-Level Gameplay

An example of an innovative use of AR technology in gaming can be found in Cyberpunk 2077. In one mission, the player uses a device called a like VR glasses to experience an augmented reality world that is virtually indistinguishable from reality. The concept of using the device to play an AR game is an intriguing one, as it has the potential to create an even more immersive gaming experience. By combining AR technology with a braindance-like device, game developers could create an exciting new frontier for gaming.

In the franchise’s movie Sword Art Online: Ordinal Scale, AR technology plays a central role in the story. There’s a game called Ordinal Scale allows players to experience the game world through the use of an AR headset called the “Augma”. The movie explores the consequences of AR technology and the potential dangers of becoming too immersed in a virtual world. It also highlights the potential benefits of AR technology, such as increased social interaction and the ability to enjoy gaming experiences in a more accessible and safer way.

However, there are also ethical and safety concerns that would need to be addressed. For example, if the device is used to create a virtual reality that is indistinguishable from reality, it could be difficult to tell whether someone is in the real world or the virtual world, which could be dangerous. Additionally, there would need to be safeguards in place to prevent addiction or other negative effects of prolonged use.

Outro

Geolocation-based games have become popular due to the advancements in technology and the rise of mobile devices. These games offer players the chance to explore their surroundings and blend the virtual and real world, creating a unique gaming experience.

While collaborating with well-known brands or franchises can certainly help to increase success, it is not a panacea. To evolve gameplay experiences, developers should explore new gameplay mechanics that utilize real-world data. Combining these mechanics with other elements and the continuous advancement of technology provides developers with the opportunity to create more engaging experiences that seamlessly blend the real and virtual worlds.

The future of geolocation-based games is bright, as developers leverage geographic data to create innovative gameplay experiences. With technology advancements, these games can revolutionize the industry, offering adventures that blend the real and virtual worlds. The possibilities for utilizing geographic data are endless and could expand into healthcare and education. The future of these games is promising.
