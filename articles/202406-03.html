Farewell to Light and Dark: Destiny Sets the Stage for an Epic Future

A look back at 7 years of Guardians journeys

Ossian Lin Published on Jun 2024

Destiny 2 has carved a unique path in the looter-shooter genre over the past seven years. Launched in 2017, it has become a constant companion for millions of players, forging a community of Guardians united by their love for challenging foes, collecting loot, and unraveling the mysteries of a vibrant sci-fi world. The Destiny series has become a familiar world, a second home for Guardians like myself. This ever-evolving title has been shaped by the experiences and feedback of its dedicated player base.

With a unique path in the gaming world, offering a constantly evolving experience for Guardians for over seven years. Bungie has continuously refined the user experience to cater to both veteran players and newcomers. This article delves into the game’s experience journey, examining the triumphs that have solidified Destiny’s position and the ongoing areas for improvement that Bungie, the developer, strives to address. So, fellow Guardians, grab a fresh cup of Traveler-blessed coffee, and let’s reminisce about Destiny’s game experience — the good, the bad, and the ever-evolving.

Evolving the Guardian Experience

Destiny 1 was first released on PlayStation 3, PlayStation 4, Xbox 360 and Xbox One in 2014, and was initially only available on home consoles. While it was not uncommon to play shooter games on consoles at the time, Destiny 1 was a persistent online RPG with a focus on loot and shoot gameplay across various modes. As such, the interface design needed to balance the immediacy of a shooter with the complexity of an RPG.

Bungie, with its experience in the well-known shooter game HALO, carried over some of its interface design elements, such as the information layout of the heads-up display (HUD) and the management of weapons and equipment. However, as an online game with a continuous service model, Bungie also introduced innovative interface design elements to accommodate the diversity of game modes and content.

Accessing essential and frequently used features like the equipment interface and map is typically done from a paused state in single-player games, allowing players ample time to browse and edit their gear without any disruption. However, in MMO games, which emphasize real-time action and quick access, fulfilling this need within the limited screen space and controller button configurations of home consoles presents a significant challenge. Bungie’s approach to these features balances traditional menu interfaces with instant access, enabling players of varying experience levels to quickly navigate using familiar interfaces while also providing real-time menu toggling without loading times, allowing players to dynamically switch equipment to adapt to changing battle situations.

Bungie’s masterful information architecture shines through in its streamlined page design, allowing players to navigate through different layers using just two buttons. The character gear configuration category, accessible with a single button press, encompasses features like inventory, collections, and settings — all elements closely tied to the player’s personal experience. This intuitive organization fosters a sense of familiarity and enables players to quickly find their way around.

The map menu, similarly, consolidates community-oriented features such as the destination, map, quests, and social. This categorization empowers players to swiftly locate mission destinations. Continuous refinements have further enhanced the system, now enabling players to jump directly to certain destinations from mission details, eliminating the need to manually switch maps.

Destiny’s interface design strikes a balance between readability, usability, information density, and social interaction. It also incorporates innovative elements to accommodate the diversity of game modes and content. This results in a positive player experience. The interface design of Destiny, from HALO to Destiny, showcases Bungie’s expertise and creativity in this area. These interface designs not only provide players with clear and easy-to-use information but also support player social interaction and gaming experiences.

Lootin’, Shootin’, and Forgin’ a better experience

As game data expands with the release of new expansions, Bungie implemented a “Destiny Content Vault” to archive select content and alleviate storage demands on players’ devices. However, this approach of content removal has not been universally well-received by the player base. Despite this, Bungie has not yet reintroduced all vaulted content, but with some items reforging in later seasons after undergoing modifications.

In response to player feedback regarding repetitive bounties, Bungie implemented a system of Challenges, which replaced the monotony and offered a more engaging gameplay loop. Furthermore, the studio addressed player requests for clearer explanations by providing comprehensive breakdowns of mods, perks, and other in-game mechanics, enhancing player understanding.

Ahead of the release of The Final Shape expansion, Bungie has announced the removal of a long-standing item — Legendary Shards. This resource, which can be obtained by dismantling gear in the game, has been used for a variety of purposes. However, the Economy team has identified Legendary Shards as a long-standing source of instability in Destiny 2’s economy. Some players have amassed an excessive amount, while others struggle to acquire enough to interact with the system. This makes it difficult to use Legendary Shards as an effective economic lever, while also ensuring that content that does not require Legendary Shards remains accessible to all players, thereby reducing disparities in wealth.

To help mitigate this loss, Bungie has increased the rewards for dismantling Legendary Shards. Additionally, players will be able to summon these items from Collections on Ships and Sparrows without the original Legendary Shard cost in the future. This will allow players to change their style more freely and casually without worrying about spending any currency.

Destiny may be known for its loot and shooting, but Bungie elevates the experience with unforgettable raids. These represent the pinnacle activity could be the game changer. Each raid features intricate, multi-stage encounters with unique mechanics. Overcoming these challenges requires synchronized execution amongst your fireteam of five. Raids offer a challenging and immensely rewarding endgame experience and it also contiune the life cycle.

Beyond the fireteam experience, Destiny raids have become a cornerstone of the game’s global community. Each new raid release ignites the World First race, a prestigious competition where teams worldwide vie to be the first to conquer its challenges. This event transcends individual accomplishment. Viewers worldwide are captivated by these “pioneer” teams, whose strategies and triumphs provide invaluable insights for fellow Guardians attempting the raid.

Beyond the competitive spirit, the World First race for new Destiny raids has emerged as a significant element within the game’s narrative structure. This is exemplified by The Final Shape, where while clues regarding the Witness were presented, the core story progression remained inaccessible until a fireteam successfully completed Salvation’s Edge. The community followed this event with bated breath, witnessing these pioneering Guardians unlock the next narrative chapter within a remarkable 18 hours. This event solidified raids as not merely challenging endgame activities, but rather as pivotal milestones that weave the tapestry of Destiny’s ongoing narrative.

Raids in Destiny are more than intricate encounters. They are a global phenomenon that fosters teamwork, discovery, and a shared sense of accomplishment. They represent a unique opportunity for the community to come together and unravel the game’s deepest secrets.

Unveiling the depth of the world

Destiny’s worldbuilding is meticulously crafted, encompassing a vast expanse of lore and intricate character development. This intricate tapestry of narrative elements is seamlessly woven into the very fabric of the game, from meticulously designed maps to meticulously curated collectibles and even the legends inscribed upon equipment. This immersive approach to worldbuilding invites players to embark on a profound exploration of the meticulously crafted science fiction universe envisioned by the development team.

Among the expansions, The Final Shape, which ties the story together and brings it to a close, is undoubtedly the pinnacle. However, in terms of plot, the standout season from the past seven years is Season 18’s Season of the Haunted. In this season, Bungie brings back players’ favorite area, Leviathan, and some iconic weapons. The storyline delves into the nightmares of three characters and guides players through the backstory of a villain. While it wasn’t a season that generated much heated discussion among players, I still enjoyed the way the story was told.

In the realm of video games, music plays a crucial role in shaping the overall experience, immersing players in the game’s world and amplifying the emotional impact of the story and gameplay. Each expansion in Destiny’s expansive universe features a distinct thematic identity, and the accompanying soundtrack is meticulously crafted to complement these themes. The music seamlessly blends with the narrative and gameplay environments, creating an immersive soundscape that draws players deeper into the game’s world.

This deliberate approach to music design extends beyond merely providing background ambiance. Destiny’s music actively engages players’ emotions, underscoring pivotal moments in the story and intensifying the thrill of combat encounters. The game’s composers skillfully weave together a tapestry of musical styles, from soaring orchestral pieces to pulsating electronic beats, each carefully selected to evoke the desired emotional response.

One particularly noteworthy example of Destiny’s music-driven storytelling is the track Deep Stone Lullaby from the Beyond Light expansion. This hauntingly beautiful piece plays during a reflective moment following a challenging encounter, its gentle melody and melancholic tone serving as a poignant counterpoint to the adrenaline-fueled action that preceded it.

Deep Stone Lullaby exemplifies the power of music to enhance narrative and evoke emotion. It is a testament to Destiny’s commitment to creating a truly immersive and impactful gaming experience, where music plays an integral role in shaping the player’s journey through the game’s vast and captivating universe.

As the story and expansions progress, Bungie also revises existing names and descriptions to reflect the evolving narrative. For instance, the Beyond Light expansion introduced Stasis, a new element derived from Darkness, distinct from the previously exclusive Traveler-granted powers. Consequently, the item related to Guardians’ Super ability, formerly known as “Orbs of Light” was renamed “Orb of Power”. This change wasn’t explicitly addressed in patch notes, but players noticed it in-game. “Orbs of Light” wouldn’t thematically fit with an element derived from the Darkness. So this change is a more neutral term encompassing all abilities that generate orbs, regardless of subclass.

A retrospective evaluation of the decade-long Light and Dark saga yields a score of 9 out of 10. The narrative arc, while uncomplicated at its core, benefits from the intricate world-building delivered through the annual seasonal content. However, the content vaulting approach has resulted in occasional narrative inconsistencies, potentially hindering new player accessibility to the saga’s earlier chapters. Despite this, the final chapter’s meticulous narrative build-up proves immensely satisfying, culminating in an unforgettable conclusion to the climactic confrontation.

Take away

Destiny immerses players in a captivating world filled with powerful Guardians and an ongoing fight for humanity’s survival. The core gameplay loop centers around wielding a satisfying arsenal, collecting loot to enhance your character, and conquering challenging missions with allies. Destiny excels in these fundamentals, delivering a power fantasy that fosters hours of engagement.

However, Destiny isn’t without its nuances. Unlike traditional single-player experiences, Destiny offers a vast amount of content spread across expansions and seasonal updates, unlike more linear storytelling games. This wealth of content, while exciting for experienced players, can be overwhelming for newcomers due to the intricate systems and expansive lore. This creates a steeper learning curve. Additionally, the endgame loop can feel repetitive, and the focus on acquiring the latest gear might not appeal to all players.

In conclusion, Destiny presents a compelling shooter-looter experience within a richly developed and continually expanding universe. Players can expect to engage in thrilling combat against new enemy, acquire powerful loot, and collaborate with friends on challenging missions. However, the intricate systems and reliance on a wealth of expansion content may pose a hurdle for newcomers. For those willing to invest time in mastering the fundamentals, Destiny offers a deeply rewarding adventure.
