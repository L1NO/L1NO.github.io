Collaborative Design into Open Source Communities

Designers in Tech - Open Source Design Workshop

Ossian Lin Published on Aug 2024

Conference for Open Source Coders, Users and Promoters (COSCUP) is an annual conference held by Taiwanese open source community participants since 2006. Following last year’s successful "The Design We Open — Network Disruption Hackathon" workshop, Superbloom is continuing its commitment to designers by offering another specialized workshop this year. They are reviving a one-day design workshop to provide clearer guidance on how designers can contribute to open source projects.

It’s my honor to come back and join with Eriol and Abhishek. The stimulating discussions and innovative ideas presented at last year’s event generated considerable anticipation within the design community, including myself. Having participated in numerous business-oriented workshops, we eagerly await the thought-provoking topics that will be explored at this year’s gathering.

With a keen interest in open-source technologies actively sought opportunities to collaborate with developers on open-source projects. This collaborative journey will empower us to overcome complex challenges and contribute significantly to the development of open-source products characterized by enhanced user experiences.

The Designer’s Role in a Open Source Projects

As a crucial link in the collaboration process, designers play a pivotal role in transforming developers’ questions into tangible design challenges. Developers often present problems in concise statements, lacking sufficient context about user scenarios, behaviors, and motivations. These details are essential for designers to gain a deep understanding of the problem and approach design from the user’s perspective.

Even without direct user interaction, designers can employ design thinking tools such as user personas and user journey maps, along with relevant case studies, to simulate real-world user scenarios. This enables designers to establish a shared understanding with the development team and collaboratively create exceptional user experiences.

The workshop significantly broadened our design perspective by introducing the concept of “Persona Non-Grata,” or malicious users. This innovative approach challenged us to consider scenarios where individuals might intentionally exploit our open-source software for harmful purposes. By incorporating these potential threats into our design process, we can conduct more thorough risk assessments, identifying vulnerabilities and implementing safeguards proactively.

This holistic approach not only fulfills the needs of legitimate users but also fortifies our software against malicious threats, thereby bolstering its overall security and resilience.

This workshop differs from last year’s in that we’ve narrowed down our focus to three specific products: Cofacts, Session and Ceno. Our team selected Session and focus the challenge of data backup. The scenario we’re exploring is how to assist users who must delete the app under urgent or hazardous situations but often forget their recovery phrases, hindering their ability to restore past conversations.

Drawing on Superbloom’s accessibility and usability heuristic review, our evaluation prioritized “Human language” and “Efficiency and just-in-time information.” Traditionally, heuristic evaluation has been employed by design and development teams to identify usability and design flaws within interfaces. These heuristics serve as benchmarks for creating secure and user-friendly systems.

To maximize effectiveness, multiple evaluators should assess the same interface. While expertise in user experience is valuable, it’s not a prerequisite. In fact, fresh perspectives from “outsiders” can significantly enhance the evaluation process, provided they have a clear understanding of the applied heuristics.

By conducting a thorough analysis of the problem, our team developed comprehensive user personas and a detailed user journey map to identify critical pain points within the data backup process. To address these challenges, we proposed the following solutions:

  1. Enhanced onboarding: Implement an informative onboarding process that emphasizes the importance of safeguarding recovery phrases.
  2. Emergency contact and backup: Enable users to designate an emergency contact and initiate a one-click backup for critical situations.
  3. Flexible recovery options: Provide multiple avenues for recovering lost data, including the ability to retrieve recovery phrases through an emergency contact.

These proposed solutions aim to improve the overall user experience by mitigating data loss risks and providing users with greater control over their information.

To foster seamless collaboration between design and engineering teams, the workshop encouraged the adoption of project management tools like GitHub and GitLab. By extending design contributions beyond traditional deliverables to include design rationale and project goals, designers played a pivotal role in shaping the project’s direction. This approach aligned with the open-source ethos of shared ownership and iterative development.

While the workshop provided valuable insights, integrating these tools and workflows into existing design processes will require a concerted effort. Overcoming the initial learning curve and adapting to a more engineering-centric approach will be essential for maximizing the benefits of this collaborative model.

The bridge between engineer and designer

During this event, I conducted interviews with several designers to explore the challenges inherent in open-source tool development. In contrast to commercial product development, open-source projects often lack the requisite resources and infrastructure to foster effective collaboration between engineers and designers. Given the critical role of such collaboration in producing high-quality open-source products, the absence of these elements can significantly impede project success.

Based on our discussions, we identified the following key issues:

  1. Communication challenges: Divergent perspectives and communication styles often impede effective collaboration between engineers and designers. Engineers tend to prioritize technical feasibility and efficiency, while designers focus on user experience and aesthetics. These differing priorities and approaches can hinder mutual understanding and project alignment.
  2. Complex technical intricacies: Engineers and designers often struggle to collaborate effectively due to differences in technical expertise. Designers may lack familiarity with open-source tools, making it difficult to align their designs with technical constraints. This can lead to mismatched expectations and project delays.
  3. Misaligned perspectives: A primary obstacle to effective collaboration between engineers and designers stems from divergent priorities. Engineers typically prioritize feature implementation, while designers focus on enhancing product aesthetics and usability. Furthermore, contrasting value systems, with engineers emphasizing technological advancement and designers prioritizing user-centric outcomes, can exacerbate these challenges.

While sharing similarities with traditional product development, open-source projects present distinct challenges. The specialized nature of open-source tools can create silos between engineering and design disciplines. Moreover, the absence of direct user input can hinder designers’ ability to accurately identify user needs.

To bridge these gaps, effective collaboration is paramount. By fostering open communication, aligning project objectives, and implementing suitable tools and processes, we can optimize the development lifecycle and deliver exceptional open-source products.

Looking ahead

My participation in this workshop has deepened my appreciation for the transformative potential of open-source tools. I envision a future where design and engineering seamlessly converge, giving birth to a new generation of exceptional open-source products that redefine industry standards. To actualize this vision, cultivating a collaborative ecosystem that fosters open communication, mutual respect, and shared knowledge between designers and engineers is imperative.

By demystifying complex technical intricacies and empowering designers with a deeper comprehension of open-source principles, we can create a synergistic environment where innovation thrives. This collaborative approach will not only break down silos but also ignite a shared sense of purpose, driving us towards the creation of open-source solutions that are not only functional but also aesthetically pleasing and user-centric.

Through continued exploration and experimentation, I am confident that we can unlock the full potential of open-source technology and establish a new paradigm for software development.
