Children's Social-Emotional Learning Platform

An in-depth qualitative and quantitative research on the emotional learning needs of primary school students aged 6–8, exploring interactive parent-child co-play experiences that integrate digital gaming with physical learning tools.

Role UX Researcher & Product Designer
Target Audience Children aged 6–8 & their parents
Developmental Stage Concrete Operational / Industry vs. Inferiority
Research Scope ~2.4M age-eligible children across Taiwan
01 / Context & Objectives

Project Overview

Focusing on emotional cognitive blind spots during critical stages of child development, exploring how age-appropriate interaction design can alleviate emotional friction between parents and children.

🎯

Research Objectives

Gain deep insights into the emotional learning needs of children aged 6–8, exploring how a blended physical-digital approach can help kids build foundational skills in emotion recognition, expression, and self-regulation.

👥

Target Demographics

Early primary school students (ages 6–8) and their parents, with a core focus on children in Piaget's "Concrete Operational Stage" and Erikson's "Industry vs. Inferiority" developmental stage.

⏰

Research Background

Investigating a demographic of approximately 2.4 million school-aged children (ages 6–11) in Taiwan, bridging developmental psychology theories with real-world digital behavior observations in modern families.

🔍 Key Research Findings

  • Gap in Emotional Awareness & Regulation: Children begin to recognize their feelings but lack concrete regulation and communication strategies, frequently reacting to frustration with tantrums or withdrawal.
  • Gender Nuances in Empathy Expression: While empathy is budding, young boys tend to be noticeably more reserved and passive in vocalizing feelings compared to girls.
  • Preference for Phygital Interactive Models: Children have little patience for text-heavy lectures, showing a strong affinity for learning formats combining animations, gamified quests, and tangible hands-on toolkits.
  • Parental Anxiety & Support Deficit: Modern parents face dual pressures from work and caregiving; over 50% report parenting fatigue or anxiety, desperately seeking lightweight, actionable guidance tools.
  • Demand for Non-Punitive Communication Aids: An urgent need exists for physical and digital tools that support parents in guiding children through moments of conflict using nonviolent, restorative communication.
03 / User Personas

Target Personas

Constructing dual persona models for both child and parent based on research data, ensuring the design aligns with children's intrinsic learning motivation and parents' facilitative needs.

👦

Ming - 7-Year-Old Student

1st Grader: Active and curious; loves building blocks and watching cartoons

Behavioral Traits: Inquisitive about new things, has an attention span of 10–15 minutes, thrives on immediate positive reinforcement.

Core Pain Points: Sulks or throws tantrums when building blocks collapse or games fail; struggles to articulate the root cause of his frustration.

Core Needs: Learn emotional vocabulary through engaging stories and role-playing; experience the joy of sharing and collaboration through interactive questing.

👩

Sarah Chen - 35-Year-Old Parent

Working professional in a dual-income household; values education but has limited evening time

Behavioral Traits: Actively seeks parenting resources; strives for meaningful, high-quality, and stress-free family time.

Core Pain Points: Exhaustion after work makes it difficult to stay patient during temper tantrums, leading to guilt and anxiety afterward.

Core Needs: Intuitive, low-friction toolkits and apps that require minimal setup, offering ready-to-use scenario scripts and guided reflection exercises.

04 / Core Challenges

Problem Definition & "How Might We" Statements

Reframing user pain points into actionable design questions focused on gamified experiences, conflict-resolution mechanisms, and positive parent-child dynamics.

HMW 01 / Emotional Self-Awareness

How might we enable children aged 6–8 to identify and express emotions playfully within games or stories, thereby fostering self-awareness and empathy?

HMW 02 / Collaborative Coping Mechanisms

How might we design narrative-driven or mission-based tools that allow kids to practice overcoming setbacks, resolving conflicts, and regulating emotions through cooperative play?

HMW 03 / Positive Parent-Child Co-Engagement

How might we create co-play experiences that guide parents in using nonviolent communication and open-ended dialogues to support their children through negative emotions?

05 / Design Strategy

Design Solutions & Strategic Modules

Grounded in developmental psychology, proposing a product experience architecture anchored by four pillars: gamification, visual learning aids, peer collaboration, and parent coaching.

🎮

Gamified Scenario-Based Learning

Provides immersive adventure narratives and role-playing quests that allow children to experience emotions like "anger, sadness, fear, and joy" in safe virtual scenarios. Completing mini-challenges unlocks instant positive reinforcement, steadily building emotional competence.

🎨

Tangible Emotion Visualizers

Custom-designed emotion monster characters, facial expression cards, and tactile stickers transform abstract, complex feelings into tangible colors and shapes, bridging vocabulary barriers and enabling kids to pinpoint their emotional state accurately.

👫

Peer Collaborative Puzzle-Solving

Designed multi-player cooperative levels encouraging children to practice perspective-taking and empathy through turn-taking, division of labor, and sharing, nurturing social competence and conflict-resolution skills in a secure environment.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Parent Coaching Support System

An in-app "Parent Toolkit" that offers real-time de-escalation prompts and positive dialogue scripts during emotional meltdowns, empowering parents to replace reprimand with active listening and reinforcing secure attachment bonds.

06 / Synthesis & Takeaways

Research Convergence & Core Value

Synthesizing research deliverables to establish a clear, actionable strategic foundation for subsequent prototyping and physical kit manufacturing.

💡

Core Design Insights

Successful social-emotional learning for young children must be cultivated within a safe, playful, and non-judgmental environment. Standalone digital content or purely paper-based kits have notable limitations; only a synergy of "phygital gamification + parents acting as supportive coaches" can fulfill the dual value of child-led autonomous exploration and reassuring parental guidance.

📋

Project Deliverables

• A comprehensive emotional learning journey map and developmental model for children aged 6–8
• Well-defined child and parent personas alongside a prioritized pain-point matrix
• 3 targeted "How Might We" problem statements and 4 core strategic feature modules
• Clear design specifications and guidelines for interactive prototyping and mass-production of physical kits
